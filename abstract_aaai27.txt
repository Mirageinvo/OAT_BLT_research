ABSTRACT (~215 words) — v4, natural voice (final)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Action-chunking policies reduce decision frequency by predicting a short sequence
of future actions, but most of them use the same compute budget at every
observation: a fixed number of action tokens and a fixed replanning horizon. We
study whether this budget can be allocated adaptively in OAT, a prefix-decodable
action tokenizer. Through counterfactual simulation under matched average budgets,
we estimate the oracle value of changing token depth and replanning horizon on a
per-observation basis. The value is surprisingly small: even an oracle assigns
little exploitable per-state benefit to either choice, and learned or heuristic
controllers do not outperform budget-matched random allocation. Our analysis
indicates that closed-loop replanning washes out many single-chunk compute
decisions.

This failure mode suggests a different use of extra computation. Instead of trying
to reduce the budget of each observation, we sample several action chunks and
select the candidate most consistent with the others in action space, while reusing
a single perception pass across candidates. This simple best-of-N procedure
improves success over the base OAT policy by up to 11 absolute points. We then
distill the selected behavior into a single-forward policy, retaining the gain
without additional inference cost. The effect holds across LIBERO, robomimic, and
MetaWorld. Overall, our results argue that for action-chunking policies, candidate
selection is a more effective use of compute than per-observation budget reduction.
